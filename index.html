O — OutcomeWhat result do you measure?

Where to Find Your Sources

Good research builds on existing knowledge. Here are the best places to look — select the ones you'll use.

Select Your Research Sources
Check everything you plan to use. You can always add more later.
🎓
Google Scholar
Free access to academic papers, theses, and citations. Best starting point.
🔬
PubMed / NCBI
Biomedical and life sciences research. Free full-text articles available.
📰
JSTOR
Humanities, social sciences, and arts. Many articles free with registration.
🏫
School / Public Library
Access to EBSCO, ProQuest, and physical books via your librarian.
🏛️
University Library
Some universities offer community access. Ask about interlibrary loan programs.
👩‍🔬
Expert / Mentor
Email a professor or professional in your field. Many will respond to curious students!
📋
Surveys / Primary Data
Collect your own data via Google Forms, interviews, or experiments.
🌐
Gov / NGO Reports
CDC, WHO, Census Bureau, Pew Research — authoritative data, free to use.
📡
News / Media Archives
NYT, ProPublica, Reuters for context and current events framing.
🔓
Open Access Journals
PLOS ONE, arXiv, SSRN — peer-reviewed, free, and high quality.
Key Sources I've Already Found
Paste titles or links to any sources you've already identified.
💡
Pro Tip: When you find a great article, scroll to its references section — those are pre-vetted sources on the same topic. This "citation chaining" technique can double your source list in minutes.

How Will You Share Your Research?

Research gets more valuable when it reaches an audience. Let's define your ambition level and how you'll put your work out into the world.

Choose Your Level of Involvement
Be honest about time, resources, and goals. All three levels produce real, valuable research.
🌱
Exploratory
A semester-long project for a class or personal interest. Great for trying research for the first time.
🌿
Intermediate
A full year of dedicated research with a mentor. Aim for a school award, regional competition, or published blog post.
🌳
Advanced
Multi-year, lab-quality research aimed at peer-reviewed publication, national competitions, or university collaboration.
